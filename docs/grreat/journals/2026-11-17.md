## 2026-11-17T16:40:00-06:00 — Kill test: linter against one whole-spec prompt

- Method: a separate agent, blind to the rules, wrote 30 single-defect copies of keep-in-touch (10 cross-reference, 10 semantic, 10 local) and 5 harmless-edit controls. Arm A is the deterministic linter, counting findings new against the clean spec. Arm B is one Opus call with the whole spec and no hint, capped at 15 items. One agent scored both arms unblinded; five hits were spot-checked against the answer key. Full table: [results](../../experiments/2026-11-17-spec-linter-kill-test.md).
- Evidence: Arm A 7/30 (cross-reference 5/10, semantic 0/10, local 2/10), two of the seven from `flow.*`. Arm B 29/30 (10/10, 10/10, 9/10); 22/30 with a top-10 cap. No false alarm on the controls in either arm. Arm B returned 15 items on every call, about 14 of them pre-existing issues; six reruns kept the same outcome. Arm A: 11 s, no tokens. Arm B: about 43k input tokens and $0.45 per call.
- Finding: the kill criterion is met. On a spec that fits one prompt, the deterministic linter does not approach a single strong-model review. The model finds defects by reading requirement text against structure, which no deterministic rule here does. The judged tier was not run, and nothing was tested on a spec larger than one context window.
- Next: decide whether the graph earns its place through something the prompt cannot do (specs beyond one context, change-scoped review at lower cost, a stable finding ledger across versions) before adding rules or genes.

## 2026-11-17T17:10:00-06:00 — Three follow-up checks: what the graph adds to LLM review

- Decision (user): test all three things a whole-spec prompt cannot do. Same 30 defects and 5 controls; one Opus call per cell; one unblinded scorer per experiment; the slice rule was fixed before any model output. Records: [change-scoped review and ledger](../../experiments/2026-11-17-change-scoped-review-and-ledger.md), [larger spec](../../experiments/2026-11-17-larger-spec.md), [test set](../../experiments/2026-11-17-test-set/README.md).
- Change-scoped review (35 variants): diff only 28/30, diff plus graph slice 30/30, diff plus whole spec 30/30; no control false alarms in any arm. On the 20 cross-reference and semantic defects the answer cited the real counterpart 2, 18 and 20 times. Mean input 3.3k, 13.3k and 43.1k tokens; $0.025, $0.105 and $0.34 per call. Precision about 0.9 in every arm. Slice median 29 of 302 nodes. The two ungrounded slice answers had the counterpart outside the slice; one because hooks reach criteria through a field, not a link.
- Finding ledger: 99.6% of reported node ids resolve to graph nodes (83.9% exactly). A ledger of node ids from four clean runs cut items shown from 15 to 3.1 and raised precision from 0.064 to 0.28, and hid 3 of 29 real defects. Four clean runs gave 28 distinct node ids, 4 of them in every run. Keying on problem text suppressed 0 to 2 of 525 items.
- Larger spec (two more Things, 854 nodes, about 130k tokens; 15 defects the prompt caught at 1x plus 3 controls): whole-spec review without a diff 12/15, at $1.19 and 71 s per call; diff plus whole spec 15/15 at $1.04; diff plus slice 15/15 at $0.13. Mean slice grew from 7.5k to 8.9k tokens while the spec tripled. The three misses ranked 11 to 14 at 1x, where rank is noisy.
- Finding: the graph's measured value is scoping an LLM review to a change. The slice kept whole-spec recall and nearly its grounding at about a third of the cost at 1x and an eighth at 3x, and its size did not follow spec size. The ledger is the weakest idea. The benchmark is too easy at the verdict level (the diff alone gets 28/30), so recall does not separate the arms; grounding and cost do. Model spend for the day: about $80.
- Next: a change-review command (graph delta, slice, one model call), then a harder benchmark with multi-edit changes and defects whose counterpart is several hops away.
